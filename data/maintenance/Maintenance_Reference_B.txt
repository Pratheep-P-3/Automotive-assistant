SECTION 1: TRANSMISSION FLUID MAINTENANCE
================================================================================

Maintenance Item: Automatic Transmission Fluid Flush and Refill
Schedule: Every 30,000-60,000 miles or 2-4 years
Severity: High (prevents shifting problems and transmission failure)
When to Perform:
  - During regular maintenance intervals
  - Fluid color is dark brown or burnt
  - Transmission slipping or hard shifts
  - After extended towing or heavy use
  - When smell is burnt or unusual

Fluid Specification Verification:
  - Check manufacturer specification (ATF +4, DEXRON, MERCON, JWS 3309, etc.)
  - Never mix different transmission fluid types
  - Use only specified fluid - wrong fluid causes shifting issues
  - Synthetic fluids last longer (50,000-100,000 miles) vs. conventional (30,000-50,000)

Flush Procedure:
  1. Locate transmission drain plug (usually on transmission pan)
  2. Warm transmission to operating temperature
  3. Place drain pan under drain plug
  4. Remove drain plug slowly - fluid will be hot
  5. Allow all fluid to drain (may take 10-15 minutes)
  6. Reinstall drain plug with new crush washer
  7. Locate transmission filter (if equipped - not all transmissions have serviceable filter)
  8. Remove filter and O-ring, install new filter with fresh O-ring
  9. Add specified amount of new fluid through filler tube
  10. Start engine and allow to idle while shifting through all gears
  11. Allow to run 2-3 minutes in Park, then recheck level
  12. Drive vehicle to reach operating temperature
  13. Recheck fluid level (should be between MIN and MAX when warm)

Cost: $100-200 (DIY) or $200-400 (professional)
Tools: Drain pan, wrench set, transmission fluid, filter (if applicable)
Fluid Capacity: Typically 6-10 quarts (varies significantly by vehicle)

---
Maintenance Item: Manual Transmission Fluid Change
Schedule: Every 50,000-100,000 miles or 3-5 years
Severity: Medium (keeps synchronizers smooth and gears healthy)
When to Perform:
  - Difficult shifting or grinding when shifting
  - Fluid is dark or smells burnt
  - After heavy towing or racing
  - As part of preventive maintenance

Manual Transmission Fluid Specification:
  - Never use automatic transmission fluid in manual transmission
  - Typical specs: SAE 75W-90 or 75W-140 (severe conditions)
  - Synthetic lubricants extend drain intervals to 100,000+ miles
  - Check owner's manual for exact specification

Service Procedure:
  1. Access transmission fill/drain plug (usually on side of transmission)
  2. Locate transmission identification label
  3. Place drain pan below drain plug
  4. Remove drain plug with appropriate socket
  5. Allow all fluid to drain
  6. Reinstall drain plug with new crush washer
  7. Locate fill plug (usually higher than drain plug)
  8. Add new fluid using pump or squeeze bottle
  9. Fill until fluid level reaches drain plug hole opening
  10. Reinstall fill plug

Cost: $50-150 (DIY) or $150-250 (professional)
Tools: Drain pan, socket set, transmission fluid (SAE 75W-90 or 75W-140)
Fluid Capacity: Typically 4-6 quarts

SECTION 2: DIFFERENTIAL FLUID MAINTENANCE
================================================================================

Maintenance Item: Differential Fluid Flush and Refill
Schedule: Every 50,000-100,000 miles (more often if towing or 4WD)
Severity: Medium (prevents bearing wear and gear damage)
When to Perform:
  - At manufacturer recommended intervals
  - After heavy towing (always)
  - 4WD vehicles: every 30,000 miles
  - Hearing whining noise from rear (differential bearing wear)

Differential Fluid Types:
  - Rear Differential (RWD/AWD): Typically SAE 75W-90 or 75W-140
  - Front Differential (AWD): Same as rear or synthetic
  - Limited Slip Differential (LSD): Requires friction modifier additive
  - Electronic Locking Differential: May require special synthetic fluid

Service Procedure:
  1. Safely raise vehicle on jack stands (both rear wheels off ground)
  2. Locate differential drain plug (bottom of differential housing)
  3. Place drain pan below
  4. Remove drain plug slowly - fluid may be hot
  5. Allow complete drainage (10-15 minutes)
  6. If equipped, locate differential fill plug
  7. Clean area around fill plug before opening
  8. Remove fill plug
  9. Add new differential fluid using pump or squeeze bottle
  10. Fill until fluid reaches fill plug opening
  11. Reinstall fill plug with crush washer
  12. Lower vehicle

Cost: $100-250 (DIY) or $200-350 (professional)
Fluid Capacity: Typically 3-5 quarts
Differential Rebuilding Cost (if bearing damage): $800-2000+

SECTION 3: BRAKE FLUID SERVICE
================================================================================

Maintenance Item: Brake Fluid Flush and Bleed
Schedule: Every 2 years or 25,000 miles
Severity: Critical (brake fluid absorbs moisture and loses effectiveness)
Why Important:
  - Brake fluid is hygroscopic (absorbs water from air)
  - Old fluid with water boils at lower temperature (causes brake fade)
  - Moisture causes brake corrosion from inside
  - DOT 3/4 fluid: boiling point decreases 5°F for every 3% moisture
  - Fresh fluid: boiling point 400°F+ vs. old fluid: boiling point 280°F-

Brake Fluid Specifications:
  - DOT 3: Most common (minimum boiling point 401°F)
  - DOT 4: Higher boiling point 446°F (performance vehicles)
  - DOT 5: Silicone-based, not hygroscopic (specialty only)
  - DOT 5.1: Polyethylene glycol, very high boiling point
  - Never mix DOT 3 and DOT 5 (incompatible)

Brake Bleeding Procedure (Two-Person Method):
  1. Locate brake bleeder screws (one on each wheel caliper/cylinder)
  2. Fill master cylinder reservoir with correct DOT brake fluid
  3. Start with wheel farthest from master cylinder (usually right rear)
  4. Loosen bleeder screw 3/4 turn
  5. Have assistant press brake pedal slowly to floor
  6. Close bleeder screw when pedal reaches floor
  7. Have assistant release pedal slowly (pedal returns to top)
  8. Repeat until no more air bubbles in fluid
  9. Move to next wheel
  10. Repeat process for all four wheels
  11. Top off master cylinder to MAX line
  12. Test brake pedal (should be firm)

Cost: $100-200 (DIY) or $150-300 (professional)
Tools: Box wrench (bleeder screw size), turkey baster, clear tube, container
Safety: Do NOT use old/dirty brake fluid - only fresh new fluid

---
Maintenance Item: Brake Caliper Lubrication
Schedule: Every 15,000 miles or with brake service
Severity: Medium (prevents brake noise and uneven wear)
Purpose: Lubricates caliper slide pins so pads move freely

Procedure:
  1. Remove wheel
  2. Remove brake caliper (unbolt from mounting bracket)
  3. Inspect slide pins (usually 2 per caliper)
  4. Clean pins with wire brush to remove rust/corrosion
  5. Apply brake caliper grease to pins
  6. Move caliper back and forth to distribute lubricant
  7. Reinstall caliper
  8. Pump brake pedal to seat pads against rotor

Cost: $20-40 (DIY) or included with brake service
Grease Type: High-temperature brake caliper grease (NOT general grease)

SECTION 4: TIRE MAINTENANCE AND REPLACEMENT
================================================================================

Maintenance Item: Tire Rotation
Schedule: Every 5,000-7,500 miles
Severity: Medium (extends tire life by 15-20%)
Rotation Patterns:
  - Recommended (X-pattern): Front left → Rear right, Front right → Rear left
  - Alternative (side-to-side): Transfer each side without crossing
  - Sport vehicles: Keep fronts front, rears rear (different wear patterns)

Benefits of Rotation:
  - Extends tire life from 40,000 miles to 50,000+ miles
  - Improves handling and traction
  - Identifies developing tire issues early
  - Ensures even wear across all four tires

---
Maintenance Item: Tire Balance and Alignment
Schedule: Every 30,000 miles or when vibration noticed
Severity: High (improper alignment causes uneven wear)
Tire Balance:
  - Required when: steering wheel vibration, tire hop
  - Dynamic balance (computer-aided) more accurate than static
  - Cost: $15-25 per wheel (4 wheels = $60-100)

Wheel Alignment:
  - Required when: vehicle pulls to one side, uneven tire wear
  - Three angles: Camber, Caster, Toe
  - Cost: $100-200 (4-wheel alignment)
  - Improves fuel economy by 3% if properly aligned

Signs of Misalignment:
  - Vehicle pulls left or right when driving straight
  - Uneven tire wear (one edge worn more than other)
  - Steering wheel not centered while driving straight
  - Squealing tires on turns

---
Maintenance Item: Tire Replacement
Condition Assessment:
  - Minimum tread depth: 4/32" (legal minimum 2/32")
  - Penny test: Insert penny into tread (if penny shows on Lincoln's head = bald)
  - Maximum recommended age: 10 years (sidewall hardening)
  - Uneven wear: Compare tread depth across tire width

When to Replace:
  - Tread < 4/32" in any location
  - Tire age > 10 years (hardening)
  - Visible damage: cuts > 1/4", sidewall damage, bulges
  - Puncture > 1/4" diameter (tire shops can patch)
  - Severe cupping or scalloping (vibration indicator)

Tire Replacement Procedure:
  1. Loosen lug nuts while vehicle on ground
  2. Raise vehicle on jack stands (NEVER just jack)
  3. Remove all lug nuts
  4. Remove old wheel
  5. Mount new tire on rim
  6. Hand-tighten all lug nuts
  7. Lower vehicle
  8. Final torque lug nuts (typically 80-120 ft-lbs in star pattern)
  9. Have alignment checked after replacement
  10. Have balance checked after installation

Tire Grading System:
  - Treadwear (100-800+): 100 = hardest/longest lasting
  - Traction (AA, A, B, C): AA = best wet traction
  - Temperature (A, B, C): A = best heat resistance
  - Load Index (100-150): Maximum weight tire can carry
  - Speed Rating (Z-V-T-H-S-R-Q-P-N-M-L): Maximum speed rating

Tire Size Explanation (Example: P215/65R15 95H):
  - P = Passenger car tire
  - 215 = Width in millimeters
  - 65 = Aspect ratio (sidewall height as % of width)
  - R = Radial construction
  - 15 = Wheel diameter in inches
  - 95 = Load index
  - H = Speed rating (up to 130 mph)

Tire Cost Range:
  - Budget tires: $80-120 per tire
  - Mid-range tires: $120-200 per tire
  - Premium tires: $200-350+ per tire

SECTION 5: COOLANT SYSTEM MAINTENANCE
================================================================================

Maintenance Item: Coolant Flush and System Refill
Schedule: Every 30,000-50,000 miles or 2-3 years
Severity: High (prevents corrosion and maintains engine temperature)
Coolant Types:
  - Green (IAT - Inorganic Acid): 30,000-50,000 mile intervals
  - Orange/Red (OAT - Organic Acid): 100,000-150,000 mile intervals
  - Yellow/Pink (HOAT - Hybrid OAT): 50,000-100,000 mile intervals
  - Do NOT mix types - use only manufacturer specified

Flushing Procedure:
  1. Ensure engine is COLD (severe burn risk if hot)
  2. Locate radiator drain plug at bottom
  3. Place large drain pan below
  4. Open radiator cap carefully (steam may escape)
  5. Slowly open drain plug
  6. Drain all old coolant into waste container (NOT into environment)
  7. Close drain plug
  8. Flush with distilled water 2-3 times until water runs clear
  9. Install new drain plug
  10. Fill radiator with 50/50 premixed coolant
  11. Fill overflow reservoir to MAX line
  12. Start engine and allow to idle
  13. After 5 minutes of warm running, recheck level
  14. Add more if necessary

Cost: $80-150 (DIY) or $150-300 (professional with flush machine)
System Capacity: Typically 10-15 quarts (varies by engine)
Disposal: Recycle old coolant or dispose at hazardous waste facility

Flush Machine Service (Professional):
  - Reverse flushes radiator and engine block
  - More thorough than gravity drain
  - Removes sediment and corrosion buildup
  - Cost: $150-300 but worth it for heavily used vehicles

SECTION 6: BELT AND HOSE MAINTENANCE
================================================================================

Maintenance Item: Drive Belt Inspection and Replacement
Schedule: Inspect every 30,000 miles; Replace every 50,000-100,000 miles
Severity: High (belt failure = engine shutdown)
Serpentine Belt (Single Belt):
  - Modern vehicles use one serpentine belt driving all accessories
  - Tension maintained by automatic tensioner
  - Replacement: 60-90 minutes labor

V-Belts (Multiple Belts):
  - Older vehicles may have separate belts for alternator, water pump, A/C
  - Check tension by pressing on belt midspan (should move 1/2-1 inch)
  - Replace when cracking visible or tension poor

Timing Belt/Chain (Critical):
  - Some vehicles use belt; newer use chain (chain lasts vehicle life)
  - Timing belt failure = internal engine damage (CATASTROPHIC)
  - Replacement interval critical: 60,000-100,000 miles
  - Cost: $500-1500 (belt replacement is expensive but essential)

Belt Wear Signs:
  - Cracking or fraying edges
  - Missing chunks or chunks of belt
  - Squealing noise (especially on cold start)
  - Visible wear patterns or glazing
  - Glazed/shiny appearance (loss of grip)

---
Maintenance Item: Coolant Hose Inspection and Replacement
Schedule: Inspect every year; Replace if < 5 years old or damaged
Severity: High (hose failure causes overheating and damage)
Inspection Procedure:
  1. Visually inspect all hoses (upper and lower radiator hoses primarily)
  2. Feel hoses for cracks, splits, or soft spots
  3. Check connections for leaks
  4. Note age (rubber hardens with time)

Hose Replacement Procedure:
  1. Drain coolant into container
  2. Loosen hose clamps on both ends
  3. Remove old hose
  4. Clean connection fittings
  5. Install new hose (same diameter and length)
  6. Secure clamps firmly but not over-tight
  7. Refill with coolant
  8. Start engine, check for leaks

Cost: $40-80 per hose
Timing: Replace hoses every 5 years even if appearing OK
Safety: Replace together with radiator service (cost-effective bundling)
