SECTION 1: MAINTENANCE INTERVALS BY MILEAGE
================================================================================

Maintenance Item: Oil and Oil Filter Change
Schedule: Every 5,000-7,500 miles or 6 months (whichever comes first)
Severity: Critical (engine damage without regular oil changes)
Procedure:
  1. Warm engine to operating temperature
  2. Drain old oil into waste container
  3. Replace oil filter (remove old, install new with fresh oil smear)
  4. Fill with recommended grade (check owner's manual)
  5. Run engine, check for leaks
  6. Recheck level after 1 minute running
Cost: $40-80
Tools Needed: Oil drain pan, wrench, oil filter wrench
Recommended Oil Grade: 5W-30, 5W-40, or 0W-40 (synthetic longer intervals)

---
Maintenance Item: Air Filter Replacement
Schedule: Every 15,000-30,000 miles or annually
Severity: High (restricts airflow, reduces performance)
Procedure:
  1. Locate air filter box (usually under hood or inside fender)
  2. Release clips holding cover
  3. Remove old filter (note airflow direction arrow)
  4. Vacuum filter housing
  5. Insert new filter in same direction
  6. Close cover and secure clips
Cost: $20-40
Tools Needed: Screwdriver (possibly)
Performance Impact: New filter improves MPG by 1-2%

---
Maintenance Item: Engine Air Intake Cleaning
Schedule: Every 50,000-100,000 miles
Severity: Medium (carbon buildup reduces performance)
Procedure:
  1. Remove intake manifold cover
  2. Spray intake valves with carburetor cleaner
  3. Use brush to remove heavy carbon deposits
  4. DO NOT submerge injectors in cleaner
  5. Run engine briefly to clear loose deposits
Cost: $100-200 (DIY) or $200-400 (professional)
Tools Needed: Carburetor cleaner, brush, screwdrivers
Expected Results: Improved idle quality, better MPG, smoother acceleration

---
Maintenance Item: Coolant System Flush
Schedule: Every 30,000-50,000 miles or 2-3 years
Severity: High (prevents corrosion, maintains heat transfer)
Procedure:
  1. Allow engine to cool completely
  2. Open radiator drain plug at bottom
  3. Allow all coolant to drain
  4. Flush with distilled water until clear
  5. Close drain plug
  6. Fill radiator with 50/50 coolant mixture
  7. Fill overflow reservoir to MAX line
  8. Bleed air by running engine with cap off
Cost: $80-150 (DIY) or $150-300 (professional)
Coolant Capacity: Typically 10-15 quarts
Recommended Type: OAT (Organic Acid Technology) or HOAT (Hybrid OAT)

---
Maintenance Item: Brake Pad Inspection and Replacement
Schedule: Every 25,000-70,000 miles (depends on driving habits)
Severity: Critical (safety issue - worn pads reduce stopping ability)
Inspection Procedure:
  1. Remove wheel and inspect pad thickness
  2. Pads should be ≥ 3mm thick
  3. If < 2mm, replacement urgent
Visual Signs of Wear:
  - Squealing or squeaking noise
  - Brake pedal feels spongy
  - Longer stopping distance
  - Visual wear on pad backing
Replacement Procedure:
  1. Remove wheel and brake caliper bolts
  2. Remove old brake pads
  3. Clean caliper surfaces with wire brush
  4. Install new pads (use anti-seize on backing plates)
  5. Reinstall caliper
  6. Pump brake pedal until firm
Cost: $150-350 (all four wheels, parts and labor)
Quality Options: OEM, aftermarket, semi-metallic, ceramic

---
Maintenance Item: Spark Plug Replacement
Schedule: Every 30,000 miles (conventional) or 100,000 miles (platinum/iridium)
Severity: High (worn plugs cause misfires, reduced fuel economy)
Procedure:
  1. Allow engine to cool
  2. Remove engine cover
  3. Disconnect one spark plug wire/coil
  4. Remove old spark plug with socket and ratchet
  5. Check gap (typically 0.028-0.040")
  6. Install new plug, snug but not over-tight
  7. Reinstall wire/coil, push until click
  8. Repeat for remaining cylinders
Cost: $30-100 (DIY) or $100-250 (professional)
Recommended Type: OEM, Bosch, NGK (reputable brands)
Note: Never skip on plug quality - poor plugs cause misfires

---
Maintenance Item: Tire Rotation
Schedule: Every 5,000-7,500 miles
Severity: Medium (extends tire life, improves handling)
Procedure:
  1. Loosen lug nuts while car on ground
  2. Jack up vehicle securely (use jack stands)
  3. Remove all four wheels
  4. Rotate tires: front → rear diagonal (X pattern recommended)
  5. Reinstall lug nuts hand-tight
  6. Lower vehicle to ground
  7. Torque lug nuts to spec (typically 80-120 ft-lbs)
Cost: $30-50 (DIY) or $50-100 (professional)
Tire Lifespan: Properly rotated tires last 40,000-60,000 miles vs. 30,000-40,000

SECTION 2: SEASONAL MAINTENANCE
================================================================================

Maintenance Item: Winter Preparation (Fall)
Recommended Before: First freeze, October-November
Procedure:
  1. Verify battery charge capacity (weak in cold)
  2. Switch to winter-grade oil if applicable
  3. Check antifreeze concentration (should protect to -34°F minimum)
  4. Inspect tires for adequate tread (minimum 4/32")
  5. Test windshield wipers and washer fluid
  6. Verify heater/defroster functioning
  7. Check all lights (headlights, brake, turn)
  8. Inspect wiper blades (replace if chattering)
Cost: $50-150

---
Maintenance Item: Summer Preparation (Spring)
Recommended Before: May-June
Procedure:
  1. Switch to summer-grade oil if applicable
  2. Verify air conditioning charging (12-16 oz refrigerant)
  3. Inspect under-body for rust/corrosion
  4. Verify cooling system pressure test
  5. Check tire pressure (increase in heat)
  6. Inspect all belts for fraying
Cost: $50-200

---
Maintenance Item: Battery Maintenance
Schedule: Every 1-3 years or as needed
Warning Signs of Battery Failure:
  - Slow cranking or clicking sound
  - Check engine light (electrical drain)
  - Dim headlights
  - Corrosion on battery terminals (white/blue crusty buildup)
Maintenance Steps:
  1. Disconnect negative terminal (-) first
  2. Disconnect positive terminal (+)
  3. Inspect for corrosion buildup
  4. Clean with baking soda and water solution
  5. Reconnect positive first, then negative
  6. Apply battery terminal protector spray
Battery Replacement Cost: $100-250
Battery Life Expectancy: 3-5 years

================================================================================
SECTION 3: INSPECTION CHECKLIST
================================================================================

EVERY MONTH - Check Without Removing Parts:
  - Oil level (check on dipstick)
  - Tire pressure and condition
  - Windshield wiper operation
  - All lights (headlights, brake, turn, running lights)
  - Gauges on dashboard for warning lights
  - Engine sounds for unusual noises
  - Brake pedal feel (should be firm)
  - Coolant level in reservoir
Cost: Free (visual inspection only)

---
EVERY 6 MONTHS OR 5,000 MILES:
  - Oil and oil filter change
  - Tire rotation
  - Windshield wiper blade inspection
  - Air filter visual inspection
  - Battery terminal corrosion check
  - Undercarriage visual inspection for leaks
  - Brake pad wear check (through wheel rim)
Cost: $50-150

---
EVERY YEAR OR 10,000 MILES:
  - Full brake system inspection (pads, rotors, fluid)
  - Transmission fluid level and condition
  - Power steering fluid level
  - All coolant hoses for cracks/bulges
  - Drive belt condition and tension
  - Suspension components for wear (listen for clunks)
  - Exhaust system for rust/damage (safety hazard)
Cost: $100-300

---
EVERY 2 YEARS OR 25,000 MILES:
  - Alignment check (pulls to one side = needs alignment)
  - Suspension components replacement as needed
  - Differential fluid inspection
  - Spark plug condition assessment
  - Fuel system pressure test
  - Ignition coil resistance testing
Cost: $200-500

---
EVERY 3 YEARS OR 50,000 MILES:
  - Coolant system flush and refill
  - Transmission fluid flush (some vehicles)
  - Engine air intake valve cleaning
  - Fuel filter replacement
  - Brake fluid flush (absorbs moisture)
  - Wheel bearing repack (if applicable)
Cost: $300-800
