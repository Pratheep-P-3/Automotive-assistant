WORKFLOW 6: NO POWER WHEN ACCELERATING / BOGGING DOWN
================================================================================

Symptom: Vehicle hesitates when pushing accelerator, lack of response

Step 1: Preliminary Diagnostics
  - Scan for all codes (especially fuel trim, misfire, oxygen sensor codes)
  - Monitor real-time fuel pressure under acceleration
  - Check for check engine light
  - Note driving conditions when symptom occurs (cold start, warm, highway)

Step 2: Fuel System Pressure Testing
  - Connect fuel pressure gauge to fuel rail
  - Idle pressure: should be 40-65 PSI steady
  - Under acceleration: should maintain pressure (slight drop acceptable)
  - Pressure oscillation (pulsing): indicates fuel pump cavitation
  
Fuel Pump Performance Grades:
  - Excellent: Steady 45-65 PSI, no ripple
  - Good: 40-60 PSI, < 2 PSI ripple
  - Fair: 35-50 PSI, 2-5 PSI ripple
  - Poor: < 35 PSI or > 5 PSI ripple (replace pump)

Step 3: Fuel Injector Analysis
  - Check injector pulse width with oscilloscope
  - At full throttle: pulse width should increase significantly
  - Short pulse width at full throttle = too lean (hesitation)
  - Long pulse width at full throttle = too rich (sluggish)
  - Measure actual fuel flow rate if possible

Step 4: Air Intake System
  - Verify mass airflow sensor (MAF) reading under acceleration
  - Should increase proportionally to throttle position
  - Restricted MAF sensor causes leanness and hesitation
  - Check for vacuum leaks (propane smoke test recommended)
  - Verify intake manifold gasket not leaking

Step 5: Ignition Timing Analysis
  - Verify ignition timing advances under acceleration
  - No advance = poor acceleration and detonation risk
  - Too much advance = pre-ignition and detonation (knocking)
  - Use oscilloscope to verify timing circuit signals

Step 6: Oxygen Sensor Monitoring
  - Monitor O2 sensor voltage response time
  - Should cycle 0.1-0.9V within 100ms of change
  - Slow response indicates sensor aging or circuit problem
  - Sensor should go rich (0.7-0.9V) under acceleration initially

Related Codes to Check:
  - P0171, P0174: Fuel too lean
  - P0131, P0134: Oxygen sensor circuit
  - P0101: Mass airflow sensor range
  - P0122, P0123: Throttle position sensor range
  - P0335: Crankshaft position sensor
  - P0340, P0345: Camshaft position
  - P0012, P0014: Camshaft timing advance/retard

Diagnostic Decision Tree:
  IF fuel pressure drops significantly under load
    → Weak fuel pump or clogged fuel filter
  IF fuel pressure good but hesitation continues
    → Check fuel injectors spray pattern
  IF injectors good but still hesitating
    → Diagnose air intake (MAF sensor most likely)
  IF air intake good
    → Check ignition system (timing, coil, plugs)
  IF everything seems OK
    → Check transmission (downshift not engaging, torque converter stuck)

Repair Priority by Likelihood:
  1. Fuel pump (weak under load) - 25% of cases
  2. Fuel injectors (clogged or bad flow) - 20% of cases
  3. Mass airflow sensor (dirty or faulty) - 20% of cases
  4. Spark plugs or ignition coil - 15% of cases
  5. Vacuum leak - 10% of cases
  6. Transmission issue - 5% of cases
  7. Engine compression problem - 5% of cases

WORKFLOW 7: TRANSMISSION SHIFTING PROBLEMS
================================================================================

Symptom: Hard shifts, delayed shifts, transmission slipping, no upshift

Step 1: Verify Transmission Condition
  - Check transmission fluid level (should be between MIN and MAX marks)
  - Verify fluid color (should be bright red, not dark brown/burnt)
  - Smell fluid (burnt smell = overheating/slipping)
  - Feel for grit between fingers (metal particles = internal wear)
  - Check for fluid leaks under vehicle

Transmission Fluid Condition Guide:
  - Bright red, smooth: Excellent condition
  - Slightly darker red, smooth: Acceptable
  - Dark red/brown, smooth: Needs attention (flush soon)
  - Dark brown or burnt smell: Urgent (internal damage likely)
  - Gritty feel: Severe internal damage (rebuild/replace needed)

Step 2: Engine and Transmission Diagnostics
  - Scan for transmission fault codes (P7xx range)
  - Check engine load at different RPMs
  - Verify engine shifting patterns during acceleration
  - Monitor transmission temperature (should be < 200°F)
  - Check for signs of overheating (high temp light)

Common Transmission Codes:
  - P0715: Input Speed Sensor Circuit
  - P0720: Output Speed Sensor Circuit
  - P0740: Torque Converter Clutch
  - P0750: Shift Solenoid A Circuit
  - P0755: Shift Solenoid B Circuit
  - P0760: Shift Solenoid C Circuit
  - P0850: Park/Neutral Position Switch

Step 3: Shift Pattern Testing
  - Test shifting from PARK to REVERSE (should engage smoothly)
  - Test shifting from REVERSE to DRIVE (should engage smoothly)
  - Test acceleration upshifts (3000 RPM upshifts = normal)
  - Test manual downshifts (should downshift promptly)
  - Test engine braking in manual mode (should hold gear)

Shift Problems Diagnosis:
  - Hard/harsh shifts → Low fluid, worn bands, solenoid problem
  - Delayed upshifts → Low pressure, governor issue, throttle sensor
  - No upshift (runs at high RPM) → Electronic solenoid failure
  - Slipping (RPMs rise without speed increase) → Worn clutches, low pressure
  - Refuses to shift out of first → Solenoid or wiring failure
  - Intermittent shifting → Electrical contact issue, low voltage

Step 4: Torque Converter Analysis
  - Listen for noise (whine or grinding = bearing wear)
  - Measure stall speed (if equipped with tach converter)
  - Check for shuddering during acceleration (lockup issue)
  - Verify lockup clutch engages at highway speed (improves MPG)

Step 5: Throttle Position Sensor (TPS) Verification
  - Many shift issues caused by bad TPS signal
  - TPS voltage should range from 0.5V (closed throttle) to 4.5V (wide open)
  - Should increase smoothly and linearly with throttle
  - Any dead zones or jumps = replacement needed

Step 6: Vehicle Dynamics Testing
  - Test hill starts (transmission should hold gear)
  - Test highway merging (should downshift and accelerate)
  - Test towing (if applicable) - loads transmission heavily
  - Test on full acceleration (should bang through gears quickly)

Transmission Fluid Maintenance:
  - Drain and refill every 30,000-60,000 miles
  - Use only manufacturer-recommended fluid
  - Never mix transmission fluid types
  - Check for leaks at: pan gasket, seals, cooler lines
  - Replace cooler lines if soft or cracked

Repair Estimates:
  - Fluid flush and refill: $150-300
  - Solenoid replacement: $300-600 each
  - Torque converter replacement: $800-1500
  - Internal repair/rebuild: $1500-3500
  - Full transmission replacement: $2500-5000+

WORKFLOW 8: BRAKING SYSTEM PROBLEMS
================================================================================

Symptom: Soft pedal, pulsing during braking, brake fade, grinding

Step 1: Brake Pedal Assessment
  - Press pedal and hold for 30 seconds (should stay firm)
  - If slowly goes soft → Air in lines (bleed brakes)
  - If stays firm but weak stopping → Worn pads or low fluid
  - If pedal goes to floor → Severe leak or master cylinder failure

Brake Pedal Firmness Guide:
  - 1/4 inch travel: Excellent
  - 1/2 inch travel: Good
  - 1-2 inches travel: Acceptable
  - > 2 inches before resistance: Problem (bleed or repair)

Step 2: Visual Inspection
  - Check brake fluid level (should be between MIN and MAX)
  - Verify brake fluid color (clear to slightly amber = good)
  - Dark fluid = age, replace all fluid
  - Look for leaks at calipers, wheel cylinders, master cylinder
  - Inspect brake hoses for cracks, bulges, or age-related deterioration

Step 3: Brake Pad Inspection
  - Remove wheels and visually inspect pads
  - Measure pad thickness:
    - > 3mm: Excellent condition
    - 2-3mm: Good, monitor
    - 1-2mm: Replace soon
    - < 1mm: Replace immediately (safety hazard)
  - Check for uneven wear (lateral scoring = alignment issue)
  - Listen for squealing noise (pad wear indicator)

Step 4: Rotor Inspection
  - Measure rotor thickness with caliper
  - Compare to minimum specification (typically 2mm minimum)
  - Inspect for severe scoring or glazing
  - Check for warping (rock car in 1st gear, measure runout)
  - Rotors should be flat within 0.005"

Rotor Condition Assessment:
  - Smooth, flat surface: Excellent (can reuse with new pads)
  - Minor scratches, flat: Good (resurface or replace)
  - Severe scoring (visible gouges): Replace immediately
  - Blued/discolored: Overheating occurred (inspect cooling)
  - Warped/runout: Causing pulsation, must replace

Step 5: Brake Line Pressure Testing
  - Connect brake pressure gauge to each wheel circuit
  - Apply steady brake pressure, note all four wheel readings
  - Pressure should be: within 100 PSI of each other
  - Front brakes typically: 800-1200 PSI
  - Rear brakes typically: 700-1000 PSI
  - Large pressure difference = proportioning valve issue

Step 6: Master Cylinder Testing
  - Inspect reservoir fluid level and condition
  - Check for leaks at master cylinder base
  - Test pedal feel during braking
  - Measure brake fluid travel (should increase smoothly)
  - Listen for unusual noises (grinding = cylinder damage)

Step 7: Anti-Lock Brake System (ABS) Testing
  - Scan for ABS codes (C-codes)
  - Test ABS activation during hard braking
  - Verify wheel speed sensors functioning
  - Check ABS fluid level (separate from regular brake fluid)
  - Note if ABS light stays on (sensor failure likely)

Brake Problem Diagnosis:
  - Soft pedal → Air in lines, low fluid, master cylinder
  - Pulsing pedal under braking → Warped rotor or ABS activation
  - Grinding noise → Worn pads (metal-to-metal contact)
  - Squealing noise → Pad wear indicator or thin pads
  - Pulling to one side → Uneven pad wear or caliper sticking
  - Brake fade (pedal goes soft after repeated braking) → Fluid boiling

Common Brake System Codes:
  - C0035: Left Front Wheel Speed Sensor
  - C0040: Right Front Wheel Speed Sensor
  - C0055: Left Rear Wheel Speed Sensor
  - C0060: Right Rear Wheel Speed Sensor
  - C0200: Hydraulic Pump Motor (ABS pump)
  - C1200: ABS Control Module Fault

Brake Maintenance Schedule:
  - Inspect pads every 15,000 miles
  - Flush brake fluid every 2 years or 25,000 miles
  - Replace pads typically at 50,000 miles (varies by vehicle/driving)
  - Rotate brakes (front to rear) not standard, all four typically same age
  - Bleed air from lines any time disconnected

Safety Note: NEVER ignore brake warning lights or soft pedal. Brakes are safety-critical.

Repair Timeline:
  - Brake pad replacement: 1-2 hours
  - Rotor replacement: 2-3 hours
  - Master cylinder replacement: 2-4 hours
  - Brake line repair: 1-3 hours depending on location
  - Full brake system overhaul: 4-6 hours
