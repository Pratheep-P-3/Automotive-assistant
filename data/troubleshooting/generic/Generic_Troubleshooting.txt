GENERIC AUTOMOTIVE TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

=== Engine Misfire / Rough Idle ===
Symptom Description: Engine runs unevenly, shaking felt through steering wheel or seat
Severity: Medium to High
Common Causes:
- Worn or fouled spark plugs
- Faulty ignition coil
- Clogged fuel injector
- Vacuum leak
- Worn fuel pump or filter
- Ignition timing issues

Initial Diagnostic Steps:
1. Check for Check Engine Light and retrieve fault codes
2. Inspect spark plugs visually (look for black deposits or damage)
3. Test ignition coil output
4. Perform vacuum leak test using carburetor cleaner
5. Monitor engine vacuum with gauge (should be 15-20 inches Hg at idle)
6. Perform fuel pressure test
7. Check fuel trim values

Recommended Actions:
- Replace spark plugs if worn or black
- Clean or replace fuel injectors
- Repair vacuum leaks
- Test and replace faulty ignition coils
- Service fuel pump if pressure is low

Estimated Cost: $150-$1,200
Estimated Time: 1-6 hours


=== Engine Overheating ===
Symptom Description: Temperature gauge reading high, steam visible from engine, sweet smell
Severity: CRITICAL - DO NOT CONTINUE DRIVING
Causes:
- Low coolant level
- Thermostat failure
- Water pump failure
- Cooling fan malfunction
- Blocked radiator
- Blown head gasket
- Radiator leak

Immediate Actions:
1. STOP THE VEHICLE IMMEDIATELY
2. Turn off air conditioning
3. Turn on heater to maximum (helps cool engine)
4. Allow engine to cool (15-30 minutes minimum)
5. DO NOT open radiator cap while hot (danger of burns)
6. Check coolant level when cool
7. Add coolant if low

Professional Diagnostic:
1. Check for visible leaks
2. Perform coolant system pressure test
3. Test thermostat operation
4. Check water pump operation
5. Inspect radiator for blockage
6. Perform compression test (check for head gasket breach)

Recommended Actions:
- Top off coolant if low
- Replace thermostat if stuck closed
- Repair coolant leaks
- Replace water pump if failing
- Clean or replace radiator if blocked
- Professional engine work if head gasket suspected

Estimated Cost: $200-$2,000+
Estimated Time: 1-8 hours


=== Brake Pedal Issues ===
Symptom Description: Soft/spongy brake pedal, goes to floor, or hard to apply
Severity: HIGH - Safety Critical
Causes:
- Low brake fluid level
- Brake fluid leak
- Brake line air bubbles
- Master cylinder failure
- Brake caliper/wheel cylinder failure
- Brake pad wear

Immediate Diagnostic:
1. Check brake fluid level in reservoir
2. Pump brake pedal several times
3. Feel brake response (should be firm)
4. Check for visible leaks under vehicle
5. Inspect brake pads visually if possible

If Brake Pedal Goes to Floor:
1. DO NOT DRIVE VEHICLE
2. Check for brake fluid leaks
3. This indicates master cylinder or complete system failure
4. Requires professional brake service immediately

Recommended Actions:
- Top off brake fluid if low
- Bleed brake system if air bubbles present
- Replace brake pads if worn
- Repair brake line leaks
- Replace master cylinder if leaking
- Professional service required

Estimated Cost: $200-$1,500
Estimated Time: 1-4 hours
SAFETY WARNING: Never ignore brake issues - safety critical system


=== Transmission Shifting Problems ===
Symptom Description: Hard shifts, delayed shifts, slipping, or refused to engage
Severity: Medium to High
Causes:
- Low transmission fluid level
- Dirty or burned transmission fluid
- Faulty transmission solenoid
- Torque converter problems
- Worn transmission bands or clutches
- Engine performance issues affecting transmission

Diagnostic Steps:
1. Check transmission fluid level and condition (should be red, not brown)
2. Scan for transmission-specific DTC codes
3. Test transmission solenoid resistance
4. Perform transmission shift point evaluation
5. Check engine performance (affects transmission operation)
6. Monitor transmission temperature
7. Perform stall test if safe

Recommended Actions:
- Perform transmission fluid and filter service
- Replace faulty solenoids
- Repair wiring and connector issues
- If internal damage suspected, transmission may need rebuilding

Estimated Cost: $300-$3,000+
Estimated Time: 1-8 hours or longer


=== Battery Dead or Weak ===
Symptom Description: Dim headlights, slow engine cranking, or complete no-start
Severity: Medium
Causes:
- Battery age (typical lifespan 3-5 years)
- Parasitic draw (electrical system draining battery)
- Faulty alternator (not charging)
- Corroded battery terminals
- Broken battery cable
- Extreme cold temperatures

Diagnostic Steps:
1. Check battery voltage with multimeter (should be 12.6V at rest)
2. Start engine and recheck voltage (should be 13.5-15V)
3. Test alternator output
4. Check for corrosion on battery terminals
5. Inspect battery cables for damage
6. Check for parasitic electrical draw

Immediate Actions:
- Clean battery terminal corrosion
- Tighten battery cable connections
- Have battery tested for capacity

Recommended Actions:
- Replace battery if aged or failing
- Replace alternator if not charging properly
- Repair wiring issues
- Diagnose and fix parasitic power drain

Estimated Cost: $100-$600
Estimated Time: 0.5-2 hours


=== Check Engine Light ===
Symptom Description: Yellow engine warning light illuminated on dashboard
Severity: Varies (Low to High depending on code)
Causes: Many (over 400 possible fault codes)
Common Causes:
- Oxygen sensor failure
- Catalytic converter issue
- Loose gas cap
- Evaporative emission system leak
- Engine misfire
- Transmission problem

Diagnostic Steps:
1. Retrieve OBD fault codes using scanner
2. Check for multiple codes (indicates broader issue)
3. Perform visual inspection related to fault code
4. Test components indicated by diagnostic code
5. Check recent vehicle history (any work done, issues noticed)

Recommended Actions:
- Address the specific fault code indicated
- Clear codes after repair and verify fix
- Do not ignore Check Engine Light - can lead to damage

Estimated Cost: $50-$2,000+ (varies by cause)
Estimated Time: 0.5-8 hours (varies by cause)
