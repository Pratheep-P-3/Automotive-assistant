SECTION 1: FUEL SYSTEM CODES (P01xx)
================================================================================

---
OBD Code: P0110
Description: Intake Air Temperature (IAT) Sensor Circuit
Severity: High
System Affected: Air Intake System
Common Causes:
  - Faulty IAT sensor element
  - Wiring harness corrosion or damage
  - ECU connector issue
  - Air intake leak causing false readings
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Verify scanner confirms fault code
  2. Check sensor resistance at idle (typical 2-7k ohms at 70°F)
  3. Inspect wiring for breaks, corrosion
  4. Test voltage output at ECU under load
  5. Replace sensor if resistance outside specification
Repair Recommendation: Replace IAT sensor or repair wiring
Typical Repair Cost: $150-300

---
OBD Code: P0112
Description: Fuel delivery and mixture control synthetic monitor condition 5
Severity: Urgent
System Affected: Fuel Delivery and Mixture Control
Common Causes:
  - Sensor drift in fuel system monitoring
  - Loose connector at fuel system sensor
  - Mechanical wear in fuel injector or pump
  - ECU calibration issue
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Check fuel system pressure (should be 50-60 PSI)
  2. Inspect fuel injector resistance and spray pattern
  3. Verify fuel filter condition
  4. Test fuel pump operation
  5. Check for fuel system leaks
Repair Recommendation: Replace fuel injectors or recalibrate ECU
Typical Repair Cost: $300-600

---
OBCode: P0113
Description: Intake Air Temperature (IAT) Sensor Circuit High Input
Severity: Medium
System Affected: Air Intake System
Common Causes:
  - Open circuit in IAT sensor wiring
  - IAT sensor connector disconnected
  - Faulty sensor element
  - ECU electrical malfunction
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Verify fault with scanner
  2. Inspect harness connections at sensor and ECU
  3. Measure sensor resistance (should increase with temperature)
  4. Check for engine vacuum leaks
  5. Test ECU power and ground circuits
Repair Recommendation: Repair wiring, connector, or replace IAT sensor
Typical Repair Cost: $100-250

---
OBD Code: P0114
Description: Intake Air Temperature (IAT) Sensor Circuit Intermittent
Severity: Low
System Affected: Air Intake System
Common Causes:
  - Corroded sensor connector contacts
  - Loose connector causing intermittent connection
  - Damaged wiring insulation
  - Thermal stress on sensor element
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Monitor live sensor data with scanner
  2. Gently wiggle wiring harness while observing data
  3. Inspect connector for moisture or corrosion
  4. Clean contacts with electronic contact cleaner
  5. Check for vibration-induced wire movement
Repair Recommendation: Clean connector, repair wiring, or replace sensor
Typical Repair Cost: $75-200

---
OBD Code: P0115
Description: Engine Coolant Temperature (ECT) Sensor Circuit
Severity: High
System Affected: Cooling System Monitoring
Common Causes:
  - Faulty ECT sensor (most common)
  - Wiring harness damage
  - Connector corrosion
  - ECU malfunction
  - Thermostat stuck open (false reading)
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Scan for confirmed fault code
  2. Measure ECT sensor resistance (cold vs. hot)
  3. Compare to manufacturer specification chart
  4. Inspect wiring for damage or corrosion
  5. Verify coolant level adequate
Repair Recommendation: Replace ECT sensor or repair wiring
Typical Repair Cost: $120-280

SECTION 2: AIR INTAKE CODES (P02xx)
================================================================================

---
OBD Code: P0201
Description: Injector Circuit Malfunction - Cylinder 1
Severity: Critical
System Affected: Fuel Injection
Common Causes:
  - Fuel injector electrical coil failure
  - Injector driver circuit failure in ECU
  - Wiring open circuit or short to ground
  - Connector corrosion at injector or ECU
  - Low fuel pressure preventing injection
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Confirm fault with OBD scanner
  2. Test fuel injector coil resistance (typical 12-16 ohms)
  3. Verify fuel pressure at rail
  4. Check for continuity in injector wiring
  5. Test ECU injector driver circuit with oscilloscope
Repair Recommendation: Replace fuel injector or repair ECU circuit
Typical Repair Cost: $200-500

SECTION 3: IGNITION SYSTEM CODES (P03xx)
================================================================================

---
OBD Code: P0300
Description: Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire Detected
Severity: Critical
System Affected: Ignition and Fuel System
Common Causes:
  - Worn or fouled spark plugs
  - Ignition coil pack failure
  - Fuel injector malfunction
  - Low fuel pressure
  - Vacuum leak
  - Low engine compression
  - Bad fuel quality
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Confirm fault code with scanner
  2. Inspect all spark plugs for wear, fouling, gap
  3. Check spark plug wires/coil packs for damage
  4. Measure fuel pressure (should be 40-65 PSI depending on engine)
  5. Perform compression test on all cylinders
  6. Check for vacuum leaks with smoke test
Repair Recommendation: Replace spark plugs, ignition coils, or diagnose fuel/compression issues
Typical Repair Cost: $150-800 depending on cause

---
OBD Code: P0301
Description: Cylinder 1 Misfire Detected
Severity: High
System Affected: Ignition and Fuel System
Common Causes:
  - Spark plug failure in cylinder 1
  - Ignition coil failure for cylinder 1
  - Fuel injector malfunction for cylinder 1
  - Low compression in cylinder 1
  - Vacuum leak at cylinder 1 intake valve
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Pull spark plug from cylinder 1 and inspect
  2. Test ignition coil output with oscilloscope
  3. Measure fuel injector pulse with multimeter
  4. Check cylinder 1 compression
  5. Inspect for vacuum leak in cylinder 1 intake
Repair Recommendation: Replace spark plug, coil, or injector for cylinder 1
Typical Repair Cost: $100-400

SECTION 4: OXYGEN SENSOR CODES (P04xx)
================================================================================

---
OBD Code: P0420
Description: Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 1)
Severity: Medium
System Affected: Catalytic Converter / Oxygen Sensors
Common Causes:
  - Aging or deteriorating catalytic converter
  - Faulty downstream oxygen sensor
  - Exhaust leak upstream of catalyst
  - Engine misfire (causes unburned fuel to reach catalyst)
  - Air injection system malfunction
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Scan for related misfire codes
  2. Check upstream vs. downstream O2 sensor voltages
  3. Compare oxygen sensor response times
  4. Inspect exhaust for leaks
  5. Perform converter temperature test
Repair Recommendation: Replace catalytic converter or oxygen sensor
Typical Repair Cost: $400-2000 (converters are expensive)

---
OBD Code: P0430
Description: Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 2)
Severity: Medium
System Affected: Catalytic Converter / Oxygen Sensors (Bank 2)
Common Causes:
  - Aging or deteriorating catalytic converter (bank 2)
  - Faulty downstream oxygen sensor (bank 2)
  - Exhaust leak on bank 2 side
  - Engine misfire on bank 2 cylinders
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Scan for related misfire codes on bank 2 cylinders
  2. Check upstream vs. downstream O2 sensor voltages on bank 2
  3. Verify timing and fuel trim on bank 2
  4. Inspect exhaust for leaks on bank 2
  5. Test converter operation with infrared thermometer
Repair Recommendation: Replace catalytic converter or oxygen sensor
Typical Repair Cost: $400-2000

SECTION 5: COOLANT SYSTEM CODES (P05xx)
================================================================================

---
OBD Code: P0500
Description: Vehicle Speed Sensor Malfunction
Severity: Medium
System Affected: Transmission Control / ABS System
Common Causes:
  - Faulty vehicle speed sensor
  - Wiring damage or corrosion
  - Sensor alignment issue
  - Transmission speed ring damaged
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Scan for speed sensor data from ECU
  2. Verify sensor mounting and alignment
  3. Test sensor resistance (typical 200-900 ohms)
  4. Check for AC voltage output while driving
  5. Inspect wiring for damage
Repair Recommendation: Replace vehicle speed sensor or repair wiring
Typical Repair Cost: $150-400

---
OBD Code: P0505
Description: Idle Air Control System Malfunction
Severity: Medium
System Affected: Idle Control System
Common Causes:
  - Faulty Idle Air Control (IAC) valve
  - Carbon buildup in throttle body
  - Vacuum leak
  - ECU malfunction
  - Faulty throttle position sensor
Diagnostic Steps:
  1. Scan for idle control data
  2. Inspect throttle body for carbon deposit
  3. Check for vacuum leaks
  4. Test IAC valve movement
  5. Verify throttle position sensor calibration
Repair Recommendation: Clean throttle body, replace IAC valve, or repair vacuum leak
Typical Repair Cost: $100-300
